## ChromaForge player handbook

Everything you need to make a character and play. Your GM runs the world; you run your character.

### What you need

One d20, a few d6s, paper, and pencils. That is all.

### The Five Founts

Founts are laws of being without agency. They cannot want, permit, punish, or plan. People and creatures have motives; a Fount effect has a reliable, discoverable rule.

| Fount   | Color  | Domain                              | Common action                     |
| ------- | ------ | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| ᛄ Seron | yellow | Integrity, boundaries, truth        | define, judge, protect a line     |
| ∵ Orvik | blue   | Knowledge, memory, hidden structure | study, recall, reveal a pattern   |
| ϟ Zhark | red    | Spark, rupture, transformation      | alter, improvise, break a pattern |
| △ Drun  | green  | Fortitude, endurance, regrowth      | endure, heal, carry, persist      |
| ⌘ Veyra | purple | Charm, bonds, social pull           | persuade, perform, connect, untie |

### Character creation

One procedure; no House, Guild, class cap, or second stat roll.

1. **Concept.** Name the character and describe one visible trait and one desire (it also says why you travel with this party). Pick an ancestry from the roster: note its boon and bane. The boon is +1 to one of its listed Founts (your choice). The bane is −1 to its bane Fount.
2. **Roll Founts.** Roll 6d6 and drop the lowest. Halve each remaining die, rounding up (1–2 is 1, 3–4 is 2, 5–6 is 3). Assign the five values to your Founts (ᛄ Seron, ∵ Orvik, ϟ Zhark, △ Drun, ⌘ Veyra) however you like. Then apply your ancestry's boon (+1) and bane (−1). Step 3 adds the archetype's +1; all modifiers stack and are added after assigning.
3. **Choose an archetype and specialty.** Pick one archetype and one of its specialties from Starting archetypes. Copy its kit and archetype ability. Your archetype adds +1 to its Fount, which is also its normal attack Fount. Your specialty gives no stat bonus; its ability text names any Fount it uses. Archetype is a beginning, not a restriction.
4. **Derived values.** Maximum HP is `3d4 + △ Drun`, rolled once at creation. Maximum mana is `2 +` your ancestry boon Fount. Armor value is `0` unarmored, `1` light, `2` heavy.
5. **Finish.** Copy equipment, roll starting coin (1d4 gp + 2d6 sp), choose one useful mundane personal item, and one mundane knack (cooking, knots, card tricks, a song; humans pick two; see Knacks). You never need a roll to do your knack well, and the GM uses it in the fiction. The GM may grant advantage or a bonus when it fits, but you can't plan on one. A knack can reinforce your archetype (drawing for a Cartographer).

Example: rolls `6, 2, 4, 1, 5, 3` drop the 1 and halve to `3, 1, 2, 3, 2`; assigned as ᛄ Seron 3, ∵ Orvik 2, ϟ Zhark 1, △ Drun 2, ⌘ Veyra 3. An elf (ᛄ Seron boon, ⌘ Veyra bane) who picks a ᛄ Seron archetype (Warden) ends with ᛄ Seron 5 and ⌘ Veyra 2.

### Checks

Roll only when outcome is uncertain, risky, and consequential.

1. GM names the risk and consequence before the roll.
2. Choose the Fount matching the method, not the desired outcome. Players may propose another method.
3. Roll `d20 + Fount` against difficulty.

| Difficulty | Target | Use                                     |
| ---------- | -----: | --------------------------------------- |
| risky      |     10 | pressure exists; meaningful uncertainty |
| hard       |     15 | expert or strongly opposed task         |

Easy tasks need no roll; extraordinary ones are hard with a cost or a need for preparation. **Take 10:** with time and no pressure, skip the roll and count it as a 10.

Natural 1 is a critical failure: consequence is worse or creates a new danger. Natural 20 is a critical success: succeed and gain an extra advantage. These override the total.

NPCs do not make opposed rolls. Set difficulty from the situation. Helping requires a concrete action; useful help or good preparation gives **advantage**: roll 2d20, keep the higher. The helper shares the consequence.

**Clues:** Correct investigation always reveals the essential clue. A check decides speed, safety, completeness, or extra leverage—never whether play stops.

### Combat

Use combat only when sides cannot or will not settle the conflict another way. Enemies need a motive and a condition that ends the fight.

#### Start and turns

- If one side surprises the other, that side starts. Otherwise roll a d6 once: odd means party starts, even means enemy group starts.
- Alternate the whole party turn and the whole enemy-group turn.
- During party turn, each player chooses their own order and action. GM orders enemy actions.
- Each character gets one move, one action, one reaction, and any bonus action granted by an ability.
- One move crosses a nearby zone or about 30 feet. Action attacks, casts, helps, rescues, uses an item, or attempts another consequential task. One reaction responds to a clear trigger; it refreshes at the start of the party's turn.

#### Attacks and damage

Attacks always land unless fiction prevents them. No attack roll, no Defense.

1. Roll `weapon die + attack Fount` (light `d4`, standard `d6`, heavy `d8`; see Weapons).
2. Subtract target armor (minimum 0). The rest is HP damage.

The archetype kit names its normal attack Fount; unusual methods use the Fount agreed before rolling. Improvised objects deal `d4` (minimum 1). Fiction can make an attack impossible or remove cover before damage is rolled.

Alpha has no separate intellect score: use ∵ Orvik for risky knowledge, memory, or analysis.

#### Downed and rescue

At 0 HP, a character is down: unable to act, but available to rescue. If every party member is down, GM follows enemy motives—capture, theft, abandonment, or rescue are better alpha outcomes than an unexplained total-party death.

**Playtest rule: rescue.** An adjacent ally spends an action to get a downed character to 1 HP; medicine or magic explains how when available but is not required. Reason: downed play must be recoverable before spell resources and healing requirements are settled. Decided for alpha: rescue needs no healing.

**Maiming.** Instead of waiting to be rescued, a downed character may take a scar to rise at 1 HP immediately (no action). Roll 2d6 (below); the scar is permanent, and long rests do not heal it.

Roll 2d6. The first die is the injury, the second the body part.

| d6 | Injury | | d6 | Body part | −1 to checks that rely on |
| -: | ------ | - | -: | --------- | ------------------------- |
| 1 | Lost | | 1 | left arm | two hands or a steady grip |
| 2 | Maimed | | 2 | right arm | fine handling or a strong swing |
| 3 | Ringing | | 3 | left leg | speed or balance |
| 4 | Disfigured | | 4 | right leg | running, climbing, or kicking |
| 5 | Haunted | | 5 | torso | endurance or bracing |
| 6 | Scarred | | 6 | head | sight, hearing, or wits |

The injury is fiction; the body part sets the −1. Read the injury to fit the part: lost left arm is an amputation, ringing head is tinnitus, ringing left leg is painful phantom vibrations, haunted left arm is trembling, scarred head is a facial scar. The mechanical penalty is the same whatever the injury, so the player and GM may play a lost limb as harsher in the story.

Each body part can be maimed once: four limbs, torso, head. If the part rolled is already maimed, the player picks any unmaimed part and keeps the injury rolled. The GM may waive a scar that does not fit the fiction. Rescue stays free and carries no scar. A character can keep adventuring with up to five maims; at six the character cannot continue adventuring. Retire them or let the GM decide their fate.

### Recovery

- **Short rest:** about an hour of safe, quiet downtime. Restores 1 mana and refreshes abilities that work once per short rest.
- **Long rest:** an undisturbed night: shelter, watch, and immediate threats handled. Restores all HP and mana and refreshes abilities that work once per short rest or once per long rest. Unsafe sleep advances time but is not a long rest.
- **Day:** dawn to the next dawn. Abilities that work once per day refresh at dawn.

Food and rations are not tracked.

**Shared meal.** During a short or long rest, one character cooks (no roll; a cooking knack makes it memorable). Everyone who eats picks one: recover 1 mana, or gain advantage on one check before the next rest. A character gets one meal benefit per rest.

### Magic

Magic expresses one or more Founts; it does not give a Fount motives. Describe intended effect and method. GM chooses the relevant Fount, scope, difficulty, and failure consequence. Risky casting uses the same `d20 + Fount` check and natural 1/20 rules. Minor effects with no risk need no roll.

**When to roll.** A spell used exactly as written on a willing creature or an unattended object, within its normal scope, is automatic. Roll when the circumstances are difficult or the use is unusual. A spell that controls, disables, or erases an unwilling creature is a hard cast; on success it lasts up to a minute or until the creature is hurt, and the creature may try a risky check at the end of each of its turns to shake it off. Stretching duration, range, or scope raises difficulty by one step or adds 1 mana, GM's choice. Anything beyond what the spell fundamentally does is not possible.

A spell cannot erase an established clue or fact. Larger scope, distance, duration, or unwilling targets increase difficulty. Damage spells roll `d6 + Fount` (+1 for large scale), minus armor.

**Mana.** Each spell has a base mana cost in spells. Work out the cost in this order: base cost, **plus the wheel distance**, then **minus** your reductions; never below 1 (never below 2 for a base-3 spell).

- **Wheel distance.** The Founts sit on a wheel: ᛄ Seron, ∵ Orvik, ϟ Zhark, ⌘ Veyra, △ Drun, then back to ᛄ Seron, running clockwise from yellow at the top. A Fount's two "opposites" are the two Founts two steps away. Your home Founts are your archetype's Fount and your ancestry boon Fount. Count the steps from the nearest home Fount to the spell's Fount: the same Fount adds 0, one step adds 1, two steps adds 2.
- **Fount reductions.** The cost drops by 1 if you cast with a Fount score of 4 or more and by 2 at 6 or more. Raising that Fount with points lowers the cost this way.
- **Practiced spells.** You can keep a number of spells practiced equal to your ∵ Orvik score. A practiced spell costs 1 less. Changing your practiced list takes a few days of downtime.
- **Marks.** During a long rest you can add one mark to a practiced spell. A mark is one stretch the spell keeps: one more damage or healing die, reach to far, target to a group or zone, or duration up one step. A spell holds as many marks as your score in its Fount. When you cast, choose which marks to use; each adds 1 mana and no difficulty. Marks work only while the spell is practiced.

Alpha numbers: playtest will balance wheel distance and practice.

A failed cast still spends its mana, and a natural 1 has its usual consequence. Minor effects with no risk are cantrips: no roll, no mana. With no mana left, you cannot cast spells.

A new character knows 1 or 2 spells of their choice. Further spells are learned through play. Cantrips may come from your ancestry boon Fount, your archetype's Fount, or your specialty's Fount.

### Advancement

Award an obstacle once when its immediate stakes are settled, regardless of fighting, negotiation, stealth, magic, retreat, or another approach.

| Obstacle | Points | Example                                          |
| -------- | -----: | ------------------------------------------------ |
| minor    |      1 | one risky scene or local cost                    |
| major    |      2 | several scenes or serious lasting stakes         |
| critical |      3 | settlement, faction, or campaign-changing stakes |

Spend points on any Fount. Raising a Fount costs its new level: 1→2 costs 2, 2→3 costs 3, 3→4 costs 4. Unspent points carry over.

**Call on a Fount.** Before rolling a check, spend 1 unspent point to gain advantage on it. The check must use that Fount and the point is gone. Use it sparingly: the same points raise your Founts.

Higher scores will unlock abilities, but thresholds are not defined. Starting archetype and specialty abilities are not threshold unlocks.

Size: large peoples can block passages, carry heavy loads, and reach farther but cannot fit narrow gaps (size never changes attack range unless an ability says so); small peoples fit tight spaces and can be carried. Size gives no stat bonus. Traits in the notes column are biology, not required personality.

### Starting archetypes

Archetypes are starting training, not classes or caps. Characters may learn any specialty or ability through play. Each Fount has three archetypes; each archetype supplies one kit, one archetype ability, and three specialties: one of its own Fount and one of each of the two Founts opposite it on the wheel (yellow, blue, red, purple, green). Your archetype gives +1 to its Fount; specialties give no stat bonus.

#### ᛄ Warden

- **Kit:** light armor, standard bow, light sidearm, chalk line, 20 feet of cord.
- **Archetype ability — Hold the Line:** reaction when a nearby ally would take damage: move between them and source; attack targets Warden instead. If movement is impossible, no effect.

- **Marchwarden**
  - **Boundary Keeper:** action to mark a nearby clear line. First enemy crossing it before Warden's next turn takes `d6 + ᛄ Seron` damage, minus armor.
- **Snarer**
  - **Pin Down:** action: with a snare, net, or cord, a nearby creature is held in place until it succeeds a risky check to break free.
- **Shieldbearer**
  - **Shield Wall:** action: brace; allies behind you count +1 armor until your next turn.

#### ∵ Medic

- **Kit:** light armor, light staff or blade, bandages, splint, bitter antiseptic, notebook.
- **Archetype ability — Field Treatment:** once per short rest, action restores `d6` HP to adjacent living target. Cannot raise target above maximum HP.

- **Physician**
  - **Pattern Diagnostician:** study an injury, illness, or phenomenon closely; you learn what worsens it, what relieves it, or what happened to it most recently. Once per short rest.
- **Stretcher-Bearer**
  - **Keep Breathing:** reaction when nearby ally is downed; ally remains down but cannot be moved or further harmed by an unattended environmental hazard until Medic's next turn.
- **Bedside Counselor**
  - **Doctor's Word:** when you state a medical order, NPCs who hear it must answer it before acting on a conflicting one.

#### ϟ Pathfinder

- **Kit:** light armor, standard blade or shortbow, climbing hook, 30 feet of rope, hooded lantern.
- **Archetype ability — Find Another Way:** once per short rest, after a route is blocked, you spot one costly, risky, or slow alternate route that exists.

- **Trailbreaker**
  - **Wardbreaker:** `+2` on checks to alter, bypass, or redirect a magical trigger after its rule has been discovered.
- **Smuggler**
  - **Underworld Contact:** once per day, name illicit help; you know someone nearby who can provide it, and their price or obligation.
- **Waymarker**
  - **Waypoint:** once per day, mark a place you have reached; the party can return to it by the shortest known route.

#### △ Bulwark

- **Kit:** light armor, axe or mace, shield or planks, tool roll, 50 feet of rope.
- **Archetype ability — Shore It Up:** once per short rest, reaction when a nearby ally or structure would take damage: reduce it by `d6`.

- **Stalwart**
  - **Keep Standing:** once per long rest, ignore the first hit that would drop Bulwark to 0 HP; HP drops to 1 instead.
- **Veteran**
  - **Read the Charge:** after one round of fighting a foe, you can tell which of your allies it means to hit next.
- **Gatekeeper**
  - **Hold the Door:** action: hold a chokepoint; enemies must go through you to pass until your next turn.

#### ᛄ Cartographer

- **Kit:** light armor, surveyor's staff, chalk and charcoal, measuring cord, map case.
- **Archetype ability — Fix the Bounds:** once per short rest, study a nearby area; you know its true edges, exits, and one hidden hazard.

- **Surveyor**
  - **Claim Ground:** action: declare a nearby zone yours; allies in it have advantage on the first check to defend it until your next turn.
- **Pioneer**
  - **Cave-in:** once per short rest, collapse an unstable ceiling or ledge you can reach.
- **Packmaster**
  - **Load Bearer:** carry heavy loads or an ally without penalty; hold a collapsing structure for a turn.

#### ᛄ Paladin

- **Kit:** heavy armor, heavy blade, banner or holy symbol, oath token.
- **Archetype ability — Sworn Word:** once per short rest, swear to protect or reach one thing; your next important check that directly serves the oath has advantage. Breaking the oath ends it.

- **Inquisitor**
  - **Smite the False:** action: strike an enemy that broke a promise or lied to you; deal `d6 + ᛄ Seron + 1`, minus armor.
- **Crusader**
  - **Unbroken:** once per long rest, ignore a penalty from fear, pain, or charm until the scene ends.
- **Zealot**
  - **Zeal:** once per long rest, when an ally is downed, make an immediate attack.

#### ∵ Tinkerer

- **Kit:** light armor, spring-gun, toolkit, springs and wire, hooded lantern.
- **Archetype ability — Jury Device:** once per short rest, action: build a small gadget from scrap that does one simple thing until your next rest.

- **Trapsmith**
  - **Trap Sense:** study a device or area closely; you know whether it is trapped and how.
- **Peddler**
  - **Haggle Parts:** once per day, name a part or device; you know a seller nearby and their price.
- **Mender**
  - **Mender's Hands:** repair a broken mundane tool or door in a minute with no check.

#### ∵ Loremaster

- **Kit:** light armor, quarterstaff or dagger, travel library, ink and quill, scroll case.
- **Archetype ability — I Read About This:** once per short rest, name a creature, place, or custom; you recall one true fact about it.

- **Archivist**
  - **Perfect Recall:** once per short rest, recall any fact or clue you witnessed, exactly as it was.
- **Linguist**
  - **Tongues:** understand and be understood in any language you have heard for a minute.
- **Spellbinder**
  - **Contain It:** once per long rest, cast one spell with a base cost of 2 or less without spending mana.

#### ϟ Duelist

- **Kit:** light armor, rapier or fine blade, dueling cloak, whetstone.
- **Archetype ability — Break the Pattern:** reaction when an enemy attacks you: step aside and reduce that damage by `d6` (minimum 0).

- **Fencer**
  - **Riposte:** after Break the Pattern, strike the attacker if adjacent.
- **Showman**
  - **Flourish:** when you win a clear exchange in public, bystanders react with respect or fear, as the moment fits.
- **Champion**
  - **Challenge:** action: name one enemy in sight; until your next turn it must target you or have disadvantage on its attacks.

#### ϟ Wildcaller

- **Kit:** light armor, focus staff, flint, pouch of volatile reagents.
- **Archetype ability — Loose the Storm:** once per short rest, action: unleash a burst on a nearby zone; creatures there take `d6 + ϟ Zhark`, minus armor, and it leaves one fitting side effect on the zone.

- **Stormcaller**
  - **Chain Spark:** once per short rest, one attack also hits a second adjacent target for `d6`, minus armor.
- **Beastmaster**
  - **Leash:** once per short rest, a spell or creature you called obeys one simple command exactly as stated.
- **Whisperer**
  - **Talk Down:** when a creature you called turns on someone, a word from you stops it for a round.

#### △ Delver

- **Kit:** light armor, pick or hammer, lantern, 50 feet of rope, chalk.
- **Archetype ability — Dig Through:** once per short rest, action: open or close a passage in earth, loose rock, or crude masonry up to about 5 feet thick, wide enough for one person, until your next rest. Solid bedrock and warded or metal barriers resist it.

- **Burrower**
  - **Tremorsense:** feel vibrations nearby; you sense what is moving in a nearby zone.
- **Brawler**
  - **Pit Fighter:** advantage on checks in cramped or underground spaces.
- **Sapper**
  - **Sound the Rock:** study a ceiling, wall, or floor closely; you know whether it will hold weight and for how long.

#### △ Rootbound

- **Kit:** light armor, staff or club, seed pouch, water skin, herb bundle.
- **Archetype ability — Take Root:** once per short rest, action: through living plants, heal a nearby ally `d6` or give them 1 armor until your next turn.

- **Greenseer**
  - **Green Sense:** know what plants and animals nearby are doing, and read from them what passed here recently.
- **Hearthroot**
  - **Slow Burn:** regain 1 extra HP whenever you finish a short rest.
- **Thornwarden**
  - **Boundary Seed:** once per short rest, action: plant a ring of seeds nearby; the first creature to cross it each turn must stop, then may continue next turn. The ring lasts one scene.

#### ⌘ Bard

- **Kit:** light armor, dagger or rapier, instrument, songbook, bright cloak.
- **Archetype ability — Rally the Room:** once per short rest, reaction: give an ally advantage on their next check.

- **Minstrel**
  - **Captivating Performance:** once per short rest, action: nearby listeners are held for about a minute or until something disturbs them; harm or a loud threat breaks it.
- **Chronicler**
  - **Songs of Memory:** recall a tale or ballad about a person or place; it holds one true fact.
- **Provocateur**
  - **Discordant Note:** action: sound a jarring note; nearby enemies lose their reaction until your next turn.

#### ⌘ Envoy

- **Kit:** light armor, sidearm or sword, letters of passage, seal, formal clothes.
- **Archetype ability — Fair Trade:** once per day, name anything you want arranged; you know the fair price or favor it would cost.

- **Diplomat**
  - **Silver Tongue:** once per short rest, reroll a failed persuasion check, keeping the second result.
- **Courtier**
  - **Crowd Reader:** on meeting a group, you can tell who holds power and what they want.
- **Fixer**
  - **Bold Lie:** once per short rest, state a bold lie; the first NPC who hears it believes it until they get proof or a reason to doubt.

#### ⌘ Trickster

- **Kit:** light armor, dagger or sling, disguise kit, loaded dice, spare keys.
- **Archetype ability — Borrowed Face:** once per short rest, action: pass as a plausible person or role to NPCs who have not met you, until questioned or tested.

- **Escape Artist**

- **Appraiser**
  - **Sharp Eye:** notice the lie, forgery, or con in a short exchange.

### Spells

Active alpha spells. Rules for casting, mana, and rests are in the guide.

- **Starting spells:** a new character knows 1 or 2 spells. More are learned through play.
- **Cost:** base mana cost in brackets. Wheel distance adds mana; Fount score 4+/6+ and practiced spells lower it, never below 1 (never below 2 for a base-3 spell). See Mana.
- **Defaults:** range nearby (about 30 ft), duration 5 minutes. Larger scope, distance, duration, or unwilling targets are harder.
- **Stacking:** the same spell does not stack with itself; the newer replaces the older. Different spells stack, but spells never raise armor above 3.
- **Unwilling targets:** Charm, Babble, Mad jig, Improv sesh, and any other spell that controls, disables, or erases an unwilling creature follow the hard-cast rule in the guide.
- **Marks:** a practiced spell can grow, one mark per long rest; see Mana in the guide.
- **Check:** see when to roll. A risky cast rolls `d20 + Fount` for the spell's Fount. A failed cast still spends its mana.

#### ᛄ Seron

- **Force shield (2):** a bubble covers you and one nearby ally; damage to them is reduced by `d6` until your next turn.
- **Reveal path (1):** you know the direction and rough distance to anything or anyone you have seen in person or in a picture, even an unrecognizable one. It shows no obstacles or dangers, and wards can block it.
- **Seal (2):** name a door, container, or line; it cannot be opened or crossed until you leave or end it. Forcing it takes a hard check.
- **Ironbane (2):** for 5 minutes, the first iron weapon or tool to touch you disintegrates, then the spell ends. Iron bars, structures, and armor are not affected.
- **Cast fog (1):** fog in a color of your choice fills a nearby zone.
- **Telekinesis (2):** move unattended objects nearby, up to your **ᛄ** *Seron* score in number. Heavier or more numerous loads make it a risky check.

#### ∵ Orvik

- **Simplify problem (1):** reveals a clue and makes one hard task risky instead.
- **Wizard mark (1):** touch to coat a target in paint only casters can see, even through walls.
- **Thaumaturgic anchor (2):** an enchanted item draws the next spell or magical effect aimed at something else nearby, then goes inert.
- **Illusion (2):** an illusion aimed at one of a target's five senses.
- **Mirror image (3):** physical clones of you appear, as many as your **∵** *Orvik* score, for 5 minutes. They have your mindset and act on their own, have your normal physical capabilities, cannot cast spells or use abilities that cost mana, and vanish if hit once. They carry no copy of your inventory, gear, or tools; clones cannot use consumables. They move and act independently, but only one clone can attack each round, dealing `d4` (minimum 1).
- **Question rock (1):** ask an object or non-verbal creature one question about what it has plausibly perceived. Only you hear the answer, and it is always true but may be partial.

#### ϟ Zhark

- **Filch (1):** a visible unattended item nearby teleports to your hand. An item someone holds or wears makes it a hard cast.
- **Gravity shift (2):** choose a direction for gravity to pull you, with no fall damage, for 5 minutes. Changing the direction takes your move.
- **Flesh warp (3):** a touched creature doubles or halves in size for 5 minutes. Doubled: physical reach (not attack range) and carrying improve and it deals +1 damage, but it cannot fit narrow gaps. Halved: it fits tight spaces and deals −1 damage.
- **Elemental hand (2):** control air, earth, water, or fire nearby. With a **ϟ** *Zhark* score of 2 or less, roll `1d4` for the element.
- **Summon thing (2):** real items you have seen within the last day, small enough to hold in one hand, appear in your hand, up to your **ϟ** *Zhark* score in number. They are moved from where they were, not copied, and return when the spell ends unless used up. An item someone holds or guards makes it a hard cast.
- **Time jump (2):** a touched item vanishes and reappears at the same spot, after a number of minutes equal to your **ϟ** *Zhark* score, with the same momentum. If the spot is occupied, it appears in the nearest free space.
- **No u (2):** reaction: flip a coin. Heads: the attack hits the attacker instead. Tails: it hits you as usual, with +1 damage.
- **Polymorph (3):** a touched willing creature takes another form of about its size until you end it or 5 minutes pass.

#### △ Drun

- **Healing touch (2):** heal a touched ally `d6`, or remove one negative effect.
- **Defy death (3):** the next hit that would drop a touched ally to 0 HP leaves them at 1 HP instead. It ends then, or at your next short rest.
- **Leather carapace (2):** a target's skin hardens like steel (+1 armor) until your next short rest.
- **Embolden (1):** a touched ally gets +1 to their next check.
- **Ghost of pets past (2):** a ghostly familiar absorbs `d6` + **△** *Drun* damage until it is used up or 5 minutes pass.
- **Rooted path (1):** the ground firms up for the party: mud, ice, rubble, and loose scree become safe to cross for 5 minutes.
- **Shelter (2):** plants and earth shape a lean-to or low wall nearby for up to six people; those inside count +1 armor until you leave or end it.
- **Tireless (2):** until your next long rest, a touched ally ignores one hardship you name: fatigue, thirst, or ordinary weather.

#### ⌘ Veyra

- **Charm (2):** targets are drawn to an object or creature you choose, up to your **⌘** *Veyra* score in number. They approach it and fix their attention on it, but will not walk into obvious danger or attack because of it.
- **Dread presence (1):** creatures nearby that have not noticed you turn anxious and skittish.
- **Improv sesh (2):** the target cannot recall what it was thinking and doing for the last 5 minutes, until it sleeps or is shown strong proof.
- **Vibe check (1):** name a nearby creature; you learn its intent toward you, and if it is hostile its next attack deals half damage.
- **Babble (1):** the target must loudly repeat your thoughts and is otherwise mute. Casting spells does not require speech, so this does not stop it.
- **Mad jig (2):** the target dances and cannot otherwise move for 1 minute or until it is hurt.

#### Cantrips

Cantrips are effortless expressions of a Fount. They cost no mana and need no roll, so they can be cast freely. Their effects are small, brief, and harmless: range self to 15 ft, duration 1 minute unless stated; effects that record or encode something last as stated. They cannot directly inflict or prevent damage, restore health, impose mechanical conditions, or compel another creature's actions. They cannot stack with themselves, bypass a significant obstacle, permanently change a creature, or do what a spell does.

A cantrip can still have indirect results in the fiction: a small light may expose a hidden creature, and a noise may distract a guard. Cantrips manipulate details; spells change outcomes. A cantrip gives one useful way to approach a problem; it does not solve a whole encounter.

#### ᛄ Seron

- **Blade ward:** a held shield or weapon shows its nicks, cracks, and weak points in faint light. It does not change how well it protects or cuts.
- **Guidance:** for a task you state, a relevant direction or visible detail glows briefly. It does not tell you the right choice.
- **Thaumaturgy:** a harmless sign of power: a tremor, a louder voice, a flickering flame.
- **Mark boundary:** draw a glowing line up to 10 ft long.
- **Threshold sense:** mark a doorway or opening; you are warned if something crosses it while you are nearby.
- **Steady hand:** hold a small object perfectly motionless where it is, or keep your own hand perfectly steady during delicate work, for 1 minute.

#### ∵ Orvik

- **Encode thoughts:** turn a short message into a cipher only you can read. The cipher lasts until you undo it.
- **Message:** whisper a short message to a creature you can see; it can whisper a reply.
- **Mage hand:** move an unattended object of 1 lb or less. It cannot attack or work a complex mechanism.
- **Read pattern:** reveal a simple repeating pattern in an object, inscription, or mechanism.
- **Memory mark:** keep one brief sight or sound for exact recall later. You hold one mark at a time; a new one replaces it.

#### ϟ Zhark

- **Chill touch:** leave a pattern of harmless frost on a surface.
- **Shocking grasp:** produce a harmless static discharge.
- **Acid splash:** dissolve a tiny amount of harmless residue.
- **Eldritch blast:** a brief pulse that moves dust or lightweight debris.
- **Booming blade:** make a held object ring with a deep, resonant tone.
- **Dancing lights:** a few small, harmless moving lights.
- **Prestidigitation:** a minor trick: clean or soil a small object, warm or chill it, or give it a flavor or scent.
- **See-through:** make one layer of a small unattended object transparent for 1 minute. It does not defeat structural barriers, locks, or restraints.
- **Reshape:** soften, harden, or bend a small unattended object of about 1 lb; it returns to its form after 1 minute. It does not defeat structural barriers, locks, or restraints.

#### △ Drun

- **Poison spray:** a tiny visible cloud with an unpleasant scent.
- **Shillelagh:** a wooden object feels unusually rigid for a minute. Its combat use does not change.
- **Druidcraft:** a tiny natural effect, such as opening a flower or moving a few leaves, or knowing whether rain is coming within the hour.
- **Decompose:** speed decay in a tiny amount of dead organic matter.
- **Seed wake:** a viable seed sprouts a little, or a small plant recovers from mild wilting.
- **Spare the dying:** sense whether a creature is alive, stable, or dying. It does not stabilize or heal them.
- **Root hold:** a small loose object or patch of soil stays put against ordinary movement.
- **Vital spark:** ease minor fatigue or discomfort. It heals no injury.

#### ⌘ Veyra

- **Vicious mockery:** your voice carries an unsettling emotional quality.
- **Friends:** a wary or indifferent creature will hear you out for a minute before deciding anything. It does not compel agreement or end established hostility.
- **Echo:** repeat a short sound or phrase you have heard yourself.
- **Emotional tint:** convey a simple emotion through voice or gesture, even without a shared language.
- **Soothing voice:** your voice steadies a willing creature's breathing. It feels calmer, but nothing else changes.
- **Shared rhythm:** you and one willing creature fall into step, keeping an even pace together while walking, rowing, or working for 1 minute.

### Items and prices

## ChromaForge GM handbook

For whoever runs the game, including a solo player running it for themselves. Rules for checks, combat, magic, and advancement live in the player handbook; this handbook covers what the GM decides, generates, and tracks.

Sections marked **Draft** are new and untested. Sections marked **Open** wait on a design decision.

<!-- art 88x55 right: GM at the table -->

### Principles

- **Founts are laws without agency.** They cannot want, permit, punish, or plan. People and creatures have motives; a Fount effect has a reliable, discoverable rule.
- **Roll only when the outcome is uncertain, risky, and consequential.** Name the risk and consequence before the roll.
- **Clues are never lost.** Correct investigation always reveals the essential clue. A check decides speed, safety, completeness, or extra leverage, never whether play stops.
- **Generated facts are canon** once they reach the table. Re-roll only before reveal, or when a result contradicts established Katharra canon.
- **Fights need a motive and an exit.** Every enemy wants something and has a condition that ends the fight short of death.
- **The world moves.** Ignored leads advance; revisited places show what changed.

### Start a session in ten minutes

1. Each player makes a character from the player handbook.
2. Roll every step of the starting-location generator. Read the visible situation, not hidden causes.
3. Pick matching names, creatures, or places from the Katharra alpha pool when useful. Generated details may fill canon gaps but cannot contradict listed facts.
4. Ask, "What do you do?" Use a check only when failure has an interesting consequence.
5. At scene end, record changed facts, advance ignored leads, and award obstacle points.

### Scene loop

For each new place or changed revisit, write six short lines:

1. **See:** immediate sensory facts.
2. **Want:** who wants what; never assign desire to a Fount.
3. **Rule:** primary Fount's reliable effect; secondary twist if generated.
4. **Ignored:** what changes if the party leaves or follows another lead.
5. **Roll:** only risk, consequence, Fount, and difficulty, not a planned solution.
6. **Leads:** at least one actionable next step; starting locations have two.

Then:

1. Show **See** and the visible problem.
2. Ask what characters do. Resolve fiction first, then checks or combat.
3. Preserve essential clues despite failure.
4. When scene stakes settle, award points and advance time.
5. Tick or rewrite every unfollowed lead's **Ignored** line.
6. Save names, rules, relationships, damage, and consequences. On revisit, reuse them and show what changed.

### Rulings

#### Which Fount

Choose the Fount matching the method, not the desired outcome. The same goal can use any Fount:

| Fount | Methods | Getting past a guard |
| ----- | ------- | -------------------- |
| ᛄ Seron | rules, oaths, edges, standing firm, honest claims | cite the law that says you may pass |
| ∵ Orvik | study, memory, analysis, patterns, careful aim | learn the shift change and slip through |
| ϟ Zhark | improvising, breaking, sudden change, speed | cause a distraction and run |
| △ Drun | strength, endurance, patience, labor | wait out the night, or push through |
| ⌘ Veyra | persuasion, performance, bonds, deception | befriend the guard |

Players may propose another method; if it is plausible, let the Fount follow the method.

#### Difficulty

| Situation | Call |
| --------- | ---- |
| no real risk, or no interesting failure | no roll; it happens |
| pressure exists; meaningful uncertainty | risky, 10 |
| expert or strongly opposed task | hard, 15 |
| extraordinary | hard, plus a cost or a need for preparation |
| time and no pressure | take 10 |

NPCs do not make opposed rolls: set the difficulty from how capable and alert they are.

#### Failure consequences — Draft

A failed check never just means "nothing happens." Pick one, or roll d6:

| d6 | Consequence |
| -: | ----------- |
| 1 | **Harm:** take `d6` damage, or a condition (see Hazards) |
| 2 | **Cost:** lose an item, coin, mana, or a used-up resource |
| 3 | **Time:** it works, but takes far longer; a clock advances |
| 4 | **Position:** it works, but leaves you exposed, separated, or cornered |
| 5 | **Attention:** someone notices; a threat or rival moves now |
| 6 | **Partial:** you get part of it and must choose which part |

**Natural 1:** the consequence is worse, or a new danger appears: the tool breaks, the lie is exposed in public, the spell hits the wrong target. **Natural 20:** succeed and gain an extra advantage: learn a secret, gain an ally's trust, finish early, or set up the next check with advantage.

### Enemies

#### Threat tiers

A creature is built like a character: its size sets its HP dice, its natural weapon sets its attack die, and its tier adds a flat bonus to both, the way △ Drun and the attack Fount do for players.

| Threat    | HP bonus | Armor | Attack bonus | Use                                 |
| --------- | -------: | ----: | -----------: | ----------------------------------- |
| fragile   |       +0 |     0 |           +0 | skittish animal, guard, hazard body |
| capable   |       +3 |     1 |           +1 | trained foe or large predator       |
| dangerous |       +6 |     1 |           +2 | scene centerpiece                   |

| Size   | HP dice | Scale                                                                 |
| ------ | ------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| tiny   |   `1d4` | pocket or pack sized; carried in one hand, slips through any crack    |
| small  |   `2d4` | as small ancestries: fits tight spaces, can be carried                |
| medium |   `3d4` | as medium ancestries                                                  |
| large  |   `4d4` | as large ancestries: blocks passages, cannot fit narrow gaps          |

Natural weapons use the weapon dice: light `d4` (beak, nip, small claws), standard `d6` (jaws, claws, slam), heavy `d8` (horns, tusks, antlers on a large body). Roll HP once when the creature appears; a fragile group member dies to any damaging hit, so its HP rarely matters.

Enemy attacks always land, like player attacks: roll damage, subtract the target's armor.

<!-- art 40x50 right: creature -->

#### Encounter size

For a first party, use at most one capable enemy per character. Replace two capable enemies with one dangerous enemy. Fragile creatures can appear in greater numbers but act as one group: one damage roll against one target per turn, and each damaging hit on the group removes one creature. Retreat, surrender, negotiation, and changed circumstances can end combat.

#### Bosses — Draft

A boss is a dangerous enemy with three additions:

- **One trick:** a reliable, discoverable rule from its Fount or biology (it cannot be hurt while touching water; it swaps places with whoever last hit it).
- **One turn:** at half HP the fight changes: it calls help, changes the terrain, flees to a second place, or reveals what it wants.
- **One exit:** a way to end the fight without killing it, findable through play.

Use at most one boss per party.

#### Creature entries

Write every creature in this shape. Finished creatures live in the monster manual (MONSTER-MANUAL.md and its PDF):

- **Behavior:** what it is doing when found, and what makes it fight.
- **Stats:** tier, size, HP, armor, attack.
- **Founts:** a boon and a bane, like an ancestry.
- **Traits:** what its body lets it do, like an ancestry's notes: how it moves, what it senses, what it ignores.
- **Habitat:** role in the food web; Fount regions (boon first, never the bane); climate patch of temperature, moisture, and altitude. Never all 27 climate cells.
- **End combat:** two or three ways the fight stops short of death.
- **Lead:** what following it, or studying it, reveals.

Wildlife wants safety, food, territory, or its young protected, not a fight to the death.

#### Down and defeat

At 0 HP a character is down. If every party member is down, follow enemy motives: capture, theft, abandonment, or rescue are better outcomes than an unexplained total-party death. Maiming and rescue are in the player handbook.

### Hazards and conditions — Draft

#### Hazards

<!-- art 40x40 right: hazard -->

A hazard hits like an attack with no attacker. Armor applies only when it would in the fiction (a shield against falling rocks, not against poison).

| Hazard | Damage |
| ------ | ------ |
| minor: a fall of 10–20 feet, a brief burn, thorns | `d6 − 2` (minimum 1) |
| serious: a fall of 30–50 feet, a fire, a poison | `d6` |
| deadly: a long fall, lava, a collapse | `d6 + 2`, and it may continue each turn |

Name the hazard before the danger, and give a check to avoid or reduce it when the character can act: a risky △ Drun check to hold on, a risky ϟ Zhark check to dive clear.

- **Drowning and suffocation:** a character can act normally for about a minute. After that, a risky △ Drun check each turn; on failure, take `d6`.
- **Darkness:** with no light, sight-based checks are hard and attacks need a sound or touch to aim. Dim light is no penalty for those ancestries who see in it.
- **Cold, heat, and thirst:** never track them by the hour. When the fiction makes them a threat, name it as a consequence: `d6` damage, a condition, or no long rest until it is fixed.

#### Conditions

Conditions are fiction with a mechanical edge. Each lasts until something in the fiction ends it, or the end of the scene.

| Condition | Effect | Ends when |
| --------- | ------ | --------- |
| prone | attacks against you deal +1 damage; standing uses your move | you stand |
| restrained | no move; hard checks to do anything but break free | you break free (risky check) |
| frightened | cannot move toward the source; hard checks against it | you leave its sight, or someone rallies you |
| dazed | no reaction; one of move or action, not both | the end of your next turn |
| blinded | as darkness, for you only | it is treated or wears off |
| poisoned | disadvantage on checks | it is treated, or a long rest |

Spells and abilities that "remove one negative effect" end one condition.

### Traps — Draft

A trap follows the clue rule: there is always a visible sign. A trap is a rule plus a consequence, never a gotcha.

<!-- art 40x45 right: trap -->

Every trap has:

- **Sign:** what a careful look shows (scorch marks, a seam in the floor, a too-clean step).
- **Trigger:** exactly what sets it off.
- **Effect:** a hazard or condition from above.
- **Ways past:** disarm (risky check with tools, hard without), avoid, or trigger it safely.

### Travel and the hexcrawl

#### Travel days

For journeys of one day or less, move to the next scene unless danger makes the route itself important. For longer journeys, roll once per travel day:

|  d6 | Day result                                                            |
| --: | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   1 | weather or terrain imposes delay, exposure, or a hard choice          |
|   2 | creature signs; party may avoid, observe, help, or confront           |
|   3 | traveler, patrol, or trade opportunity with a concrete want           |
|   4 | local Fount phenomenon with a discoverable rule                       |
|   5 | an ignored lead or faction advances into the route                    |
|   6 | uneventful travel; ask one character what the road reveals about them |

Adapt the result to terrain and Katharra content. Food and rations are not tracked. The encounter generator gives more specific road events.

#### Hexcrawl — Draft

One hex is one day of travel on foot, about 12 miles across. Mounts cover the same hex faster but still roll the travel day.

**Entering an unexplored hex:**

1. Roll a world tile: color, biome, and contents.
2. The tile's color is the Fount the land leans toward. Give it one visible sign of that Fount's core rule (from step 1 of the starting-location generator).
3. Roll the travel day.
4. Place the contents. The party sees the first one on arrival; the rest are found by exploring, or later.
5. Write the hex on the map with one line per content, and one **Ignored** line for anything left alone.

**Entering an explored hex:** roll only the travel day, and advance anything ignored there.

**Getting lost:** off a road, in bad weather or strange terrain, the navigator makes a risky ∵ Orvik check. On failure, the party loses half a day, or arrives in a neighboring hex the GM chooses (or rolls: d6, one per side).

### Treasure and rewards — Draft

Coin is not the main reward: obstacle points, allies, knowledge, and access are. When treasure is found, use a size:

| Size | Coin | Or instead |
| ---- | ---- | ---------- |
| small | `2d6` sp | a trinket, a meal's worth of supplies, a healing draught |
| medium | `2d6` gp | a fine item, a map, a book, a useful favor |
| large | `2d6 × 10` gp | an exceptional item, a mount, a rare spell component, a property |

As a rule of thumb, a session's main obstacle should pay enough for one meaningful purchase: a healing draught, a better weapon, or a few nights at an inn. Use the quest generator's reward table for jobs.

**Spirit Orbs** drop only on rare deaths: a boss, a creature tied to a Fount, a story death. They are consumed for Fount points and cannot be bought.

**Open:** magic items. None exist yet.

### Generators

- **Roll the steps top to bottom** and read each result from the table with the same label.
- **A depends entry** means that table is a grid: the earlier result picks the row, and this die picks the column. Example: the building generator's category result picks the row, then the d9 picks the building.

### Solo play — Draft

ChromaForge can run without a GM. The player plays their character and asks the dice what the GM would decide. Be honest with yourself: name the risk before you roll, and take the interesting answer over the safe one.

#### Asking the oracle

Ask a yes/no question. Choose the odds, then roll d6:

| Odds | Yes on |
| ---- | ------ |
| likely | 3–6 |
| even | 4–6 |
| unlikely | 5–6 |

<!-- art 40x40 right: dice -->

A **6** is "yes, and": something extra helps. A **1** is "no, and": something extra goes wrong. If the answer would contradict established facts, take the closest answer that doesn't.

For open questions ("What does she want?"), roll the quest generator's goal, or a d6 on the six immediate problems from the starting location, and read the result loosely.

#### Surprises

At the start of each scene, roll d6:

| d6 | Scene |
| -: | ----- |
| 1 | **Interrupted:** something else happens first. Roll the travel-day table, or advance an ignored lead into this scene |
| 2 | **Altered:** the scene happens, with one detail changed. Ask the oracle what, or add the local Fount's twist |
| 3–6 | as expected |

#### Playing the scene loop solo

- **See, Rule, Leads:** come from the generators.
- **Want:** roll it for every person on stage before you decide what your character does.
- **Roll:** name the consequence first, using the failure table above.
- **Enemies in combat:** they follow their motive. Each enemy turn, ask the oracle "Does it press the attack?" (likely when winning, unlikely when hurt). On no, it uses its fight-ending condition: flees, bargains, or grabs what it came for.

### Advancement pacing — Open

Obstacle points are awarded per the player handbook (minor 1, major 2, critical 3). Not yet decided:

- How many points a typical session should award. A session with one major and two minor obstacles gives 4.
- What Fount scores unlock. 4 and 6 already lower spell costs; ability thresholds wait on playtest data.

### Downtime — Open

The player handbook says changing practiced spells takes "a few days of downtime." Downtime has no rules yet. Candidates: train (practiced spells), work (earn coin by knack or archetype), craft (spell components, gear), research (one true fact, like I Read About This), and carouse (a contact, a rumor, and a consequence).
