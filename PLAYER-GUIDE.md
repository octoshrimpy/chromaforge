# ChromaForge player guide

Everything you need to make a character and play. Your GM runs the world; you run your character.

## What you need

One d20, a few d6s, paper, and pencils. That is all.

## The Five Founts

Founts are laws of being without agency. They cannot want, permit, punish, or plan. People and creatures have motives; a Fount effect has a reliable, discoverable rule.

| Fount   | Color  | Domain                              | Common action                     |
| ------- | ------ | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| ᛄ Seron | yellow | Integrity, boundaries, truth        | define, judge, protect a line     |
| ∵ Orvik | blue   | Knowledge, memory, hidden structure | study, recall, reveal a pattern   |
| ϟ Zhark | red    | Spark, rupture, transformation      | alter, improvise, break a pattern |
| △ Drun  | green  | Fortitude, endurance, regrowth      | endure, heal, carry, persist      |
| ⌘ Veyra | purple | Charm, bonds, social pull           | persuade, perform, connect, untie |

## Character creation

One procedure; no House, Guild, class cap, or second stat roll.

1. **Concept.** Name the character and describe one visible trait and one desire (it also says why you travel with this party). Pick an ancestry from the roster: note its boon and bane. The boon is +1 to one of its listed Founts (your choice). The bane is −1 to its bane Fount.
2. **Roll Founts.** Roll 6d6 and drop the lowest. Halve each remaining die, rounding up (1–2 is 1, 3–4 is 2, 5–6 is 3). Assign the five values to your Founts (ᛄ Seron, ∵ Orvik, ϟ Zhark, △ Drun, ⌘ Veyra) however you like. Then apply your ancestry's boon (+1) and bane (−1). Step 3 adds the archetype's +1; all modifiers stack and are added after assigning.
3. **Choose an archetype and specialty.** Pick one archetype and one of its specialties from Starting archetypes. Copy its kit and archetype ability. Your archetype adds +1 to its Fount, which is also its normal attack Fount. Your specialty gives no stat bonus; its ability text names any Fount it uses. Archetype is a beginning, not a restriction.
4. **Derived values.** Maximum HP is `6 + △ Drun`. Maximum mana is `3 + ∵ Orvik`. Armor value is `0` unarmored, `1` light, `2` heavy.
5. **Finish.** Copy equipment, choose one useful mundane personal item, and one mundane knack (cooking, knots, card tricks, a song). A knack gives no bonus, but you never need a roll to do it well and the GM uses it in the fiction.

Example: rolls `6, 2, 4, 1, 5, 3` drop the 1 and halve to `3, 1, 2, 3, 2`; assigned as ᛄ Seron 3, ∵ Orvik 2, ϟ Zhark 1, △ Drun 2, ⌘ Veyra 3. An elf (ᛄ Seron boon, ⌘ Veyra bane) who picks a ᛄ Seron archetype (Warden) ends with ᛄ Seron 5 and ⌘ Veyra 2.

## Checks

Roll only when outcome is uncertain, risky, and consequential.

1. GM names the risk and consequence before the roll.
2. Choose the Fount matching the method, not the desired outcome. Players may propose another method.
3. Roll `d20 + Fount` against difficulty.

| Difficulty | Target | Use                                     |
| ---------- | -----: | --------------------------------------- |
| risky      |     10 | pressure exists; meaningful uncertainty |
| hard       |     15 | expert or strongly opposed task         |

Easy tasks need no roll; extraordinary ones are hard with a cost or a need for preparation. **Take 10:** with time and no pressure, skip the roll and count it as a 10.

Natural 1 is a critical failure: consequence is worse or creates a new danger. Natural 20 is a critical success: succeed and gain an extra advantage. These override the total.

NPCs do not make opposed rolls. Set difficulty from the situation. Helping requires a concrete action; useful help or good preparation gives **advantage**: roll 2d20, keep the higher. The helper shares the consequence.

**Clues:** Correct investigation always reveals the essential clue. A check decides speed, safety, completeness, or extra leverage—never whether play stops.

## Combat

Use combat only when sides cannot or will not settle the conflict another way. Enemies need a motive and a condition that ends the fight.

### Start and turns

- If one side surprises the other, that side starts. Otherwise roll a d6 once: odd means party starts, even means enemy group starts.
- Alternate the whole party turn and the whole enemy-group turn.
- During party turn, each player chooses their own order and action. GM orders enemy actions.
- Each character gets one move, one action, one reaction, and any bonus action granted by an ability.
- One move crosses a nearby zone or about 30 feet. Action attacks, casts, helps, rescues, uses an item, or attempts another consequential task. One reaction responds to a clear trigger; it refreshes at the start of the party's turn.

### Attacks and damage

Attacks always land unless fiction prevents them. No attack roll, no Defense.

1. Roll `d6 + attack Fount` (+1 for a heavy two-handed weapon).
2. Subtract target armor (minimum 0). The rest is HP damage.

The archetype kit names its normal attack Fount; unusual methods use the Fount agreed before rolling. Improvised harmless objects deal `d6 − 2` (minimum 1). Fiction can make an attack impossible or remove cover before damage is rolled.

Alpha has no separate intellect score: use ∵ Orvik for risky knowledge, memory, or analysis.

### Downed and rescue

At 0 HP, a character is down: unable to act, but available to rescue. If every party member is down, GM follows enemy motives—capture, theft, abandonment, or rescue are better alpha outcomes than an unexplained total-party death.

**Playtest rule: rescue.** An adjacent ally spends an action to get a downed character to 1 HP; medicine or magic explains how when available but is not required. Reason: downed play must be recoverable before spell resources and healing requirements are settled. Decided for alpha: rescue needs no healing.

**Maiming.** Instead of waiting to be rescued, a downed character may take a scar to rise at 1 HP immediately (no action). Roll 2d6 (below); the scar is permanent, and long rests do not heal it.

Roll 2d6. The first die is the injury, the second the body part.

| d6 | Injury | | d6 | Body part | −1 to checks that rely on |
| -: | ------ | - | -: | --------- | ------------------------- |
| 1 | Lost | | 1 | left arm | two hands or a steady grip |
| 2 | Maimed | | 2 | right arm | fine handling or a strong swing |
| 3 | Ringing | | 3 | left leg | speed or balance |
| 4 | Disfigured | | 4 | right leg | running, climbing, or kicking |
| 5 | Haunted | | 5 | torso | endurance or bracing |
| 6 | Scarred | | 6 | head | sight, hearing, or wits |

The injury is fiction; the body part sets the −1. Read the injury to fit the part: lost left arm is an amputation, ringing head is tinnitus, ringing left leg is painful phantom vibrations, haunted left arm is trembling, scarred head is a facial scar. The mechanical penalty is the same whatever the injury, so the player and GM may play a lost limb as harsher in the story.

Each body part can be maimed once: four limbs, torso, head. If the part rolled is already maimed, the player picks any unmaimed part and keeps the injury rolled. The GM may waive a scar that does not fit the fiction. Rescue stays free and carries no scar. A character can keep adventuring with up to five maims; at six the character cannot continue adventuring. Retire them or let the GM decide their fate.

## Recovery

- **Short rest:** about an hour of safe, quiet downtime. Restores 1 mana and refreshes abilities that work once per short rest.
- **Long rest:** an undisturbed night: shelter, watch, and immediate threats handled. Restores all HP and mana and refreshes abilities that work once per short rest or once per long rest. Unsafe sleep advances time but is not a long rest.
- **Day:** dawn to the next dawn. Abilities that work once per day refresh at dawn.

Food and rations are not tracked.

**Shared meal.** During a short or long rest, one character cooks (no roll; a cooking knack makes it memorable). Everyone who eats picks one: recover 1 mana, or gain advantage on one check before the next rest. A character gets one meal benefit per rest.

## Magic

Magic expresses one or more Founts; it does not give a Fount motives. Describe intended effect and method. GM chooses the relevant Fount, scope, difficulty, and failure consequence. Risky casting uses the same `d20 + Fount` check and natural 1/20 rules. Minor effects with no risk need no roll.

**When to roll.** A spell used exactly as written on a willing creature or an unattended object, within its normal scope, is automatic. Roll when the circumstances are difficult or the use is unusual. A spell that controls, disables, or erases an unwilling creature is a hard cast; on success it lasts up to a minute or until the creature is hurt, and the creature may try a risky check at the end of each of its turns to shake it off. Stretching duration, range, or scope raises difficulty by one step or adds 1 mana, GM's choice. Anything beyond what the spell fundamentally does is not possible.

A spell cannot erase an established clue or fact. Larger scope, distance, duration, or unwilling targets increase difficulty. Damage spells roll `d6 + Fount` (+1 for large scale), minus armor.

**Mana.** Each spell has a base mana cost in spells. The cost drops by 1 if you cast with a Fount score of 4 or more and by 2 at 6 or more, never below 1 (never below 2 for a base-3 spell). A failed cast still spends its mana, and a natural 1 has its usual consequence. Minor effects with no risk are cantrips: no roll, no mana. With no mana left, you cannot cast spells.

A new character knows 1 or 2 spells of their choice. Further spells are learned through play.

## Advancement

Award an obstacle once when its immediate stakes are settled, regardless of fighting, negotiation, stealth, magic, retreat, or another approach.

| Obstacle | Points | Example                                          |
| -------- | -----: | ------------------------------------------------ |
| minor    |      1 | one risky scene or local cost                    |
| major    |      2 | several scenes or serious lasting stakes         |
| critical |      3 | settlement, faction, or campaign-changing stakes |

Spend points on any Fount. Raising a Fount costs its new level: 1→2 costs 2, 2→3 costs 3, 3→4 costs 4. Unspent points carry over.

Higher scores will unlock abilities, but thresholds are not defined. Starting archetype and specialty abilities are not threshold unlocks.

Size gives no stat bonus. Traits in the notes column are biology, not required personality.

## Starting archetypes

Archetypes are starting training, not classes or caps. Characters may learn any specialty or ability through play. Each Fount has three archetypes; each archetype supplies one kit, one archetype ability, and three specialties: one of its own Fount and one of each of the two Founts opposite it on the wheel (yellow, blue, red, green, purple). Your archetype gives +1 to its Fount; specialties give no stat bonus.

### ᛄ Warden

- **Kit:** light armor, standard bow (heavy), light sidearm, chalk line, 20 feet of cord.
- **Archetype ability — Hold the Line:** reaction when a nearby ally would take damage: move between them and source; attack targets Warden instead. If movement is impossible, no effect.

<br>

- **Marchwarden**
  - **Boundary Keeper:** action to mark a nearby clear line. First enemy crossing it before Warden's next turn takes `d6 + ᛄ Seron` damage, minus armor.
- **Snarer**
  - **Pin Down:** action: with a snare, net, or cord, a nearby creature is held in place until it succeeds a risky check to break free.
- **Shieldbearer**
  - **Shield Wall:** action: brace; allies behind you count +1 armor until your next turn.

### ∵ Medic

- **Kit:** light armor, light staff or blade, bandages, splint, bitter antiseptic, notebook.
- **Archetype ability — Field Treatment:** once per short rest, action restores `d6` HP to adjacent living target. Cannot raise target above maximum HP.

<br>

- **Physician**
  - **Pattern Diagnostician:** study an injury, illness, or phenomenon closely; you learn what worsens it, what relieves it, or what happened to it most recently. Once per short rest.
- **Stretcher-Bearer**
  - **Keep Breathing:** reaction when nearby ally is downed; ally remains down but cannot be moved or further harmed by an unattended environmental hazard until Medic's next turn.
- **Bedside Counselor**
  - **Doctor's Word:** when you state a medical order, NPCs who hear it must answer it before acting on a conflicting one.

### ϟ Pathfinder

- **Kit:** light armor, standard blade or shortbow (heavy), climbing hook, 30 feet of rope, hooded lantern.
- **Archetype ability — Find Another Way:** once per short rest, after a route is blocked, you spot one costly, risky, or slow alternate route that exists.

<br>

- **Trailbreaker**
  - **Wardbreaker:** `+2` on checks to alter, bypass, or redirect a magical trigger after its rule has been discovered.
- **Smuggler**
  - **Underworld Contact:** once per day, name illicit help; you know someone nearby who can provide it, and their price or obligation.
- **Waymarker**
  - **Waypoint:** once per day, mark a place you have reached; the party can return to it by the shortest known route.

### △ Bulwark

- **Kit:** light armor, heavy maul (heavy), shield or planks, tool roll, 50 feet of rope.
- **Archetype ability — Shore It Up:** once per short rest, reaction when a nearby ally or structure would take damage: reduce it by `d6`.

<br>

- **Stalwart**
  - **Keep Standing:** once per long rest, ignore the first hit that would drop Bulwark to 0 HP; HP drops to 1 instead.
- **Veteran**
  - **Read the Charge:** after one round of fighting a foe, you can tell which of your allies it means to hit next.
- **Gatekeeper**
  - **Hold the Door:** action: hold a chokepoint; enemies must go through you to pass until your next turn.

### ᛄ Cartographer

- **Kit:** light armor, surveyor's staff (heavy), chalk and charcoal, measuring cord, map case.
- **Archetype ability — Fix the Bounds:** once per short rest, study a nearby area; you know its true edges, exits, and one hidden hazard.

<br>

- **Surveyor**
  - **Claim Ground:** action: declare a nearby zone yours; allies in it have advantage on the first check to defend it until your next turn.
- **Pioneer**
  - **Cave-in:** once per short rest, collapse an unstable ceiling or ledge you can reach.
- **Packmaster**
  - **Load Bearer:** carry heavy loads or an ally without penalty; hold a collapsing structure for a turn.

### ᛄ Paladin

- **Kit:** heavy armor, heavy blade (heavy), banner or holy symbol, oath token.
- **Archetype ability — Sworn Word:** once per short rest, swear to protect or reach one thing; your next important check that directly serves the oath has advantage. Breaking the oath ends it.

<br>

- **Inquisitor**
  - **Smite the False:** action: strike an enemy that broke a promise or lied to you; deal `d6 + ᛄ Seron + 1`, minus armor.
- **Crusader**
  - **Unbroken:** once per long rest, ignore a penalty from fear, pain, or charm until the scene ends.
- **Zealot**
  - **Zeal:** once per long rest, when an ally is downed, make an immediate attack.

### ∵ Tinkerer

- **Kit:** light armor, spring-gun, toolkit, springs and wire, hooded lantern.
- **Archetype ability — Jury Device:** once per short rest, action: build a small gadget from scrap that does one simple thing until your next rest.

<br>

- **Trapsmith**
  - **Trap Sense:** study a device or area closely; you know whether it is trapped and how.
- **Peddler**
  - **Haggle Parts:** once per day, name a part or device; you know a seller nearby and their price.
- **Mender**
  - **Mender's Hands:** repair a broken mundane tool or door in a minute with no check.

### ∵ Loremaster

- **Kit:** light armor, quarterstaff or dagger, travel library, ink and quill, scroll case.
- **Archetype ability — I Read About This:** once per short rest, name a creature, place, or custom; you recall one true fact about it.

<br>

- **Archivist**
  - **Perfect Recall:** once per short rest, recall any fact or clue you witnessed, exactly as it was.
- **Linguist**
  - **Tongues:** understand and be understood in any language you have heard for a minute.
- **Spellbinder**
  - **Contain It:** once per long rest, cast one spell with a base cost of 2 or less without spending mana.

### ϟ Duelist

- **Kit:** light armor, rapier or fine blade, dueling cloak, whetstone.

<br>

- **Fencer**
  - **Riposte:** after Break the Pattern, strike the attacker if adjacent.

  - **Flourish:** when you win a clear exchange in public, bystanders react with respect or fear, as the moment fits.
- **Champion**
  - **Challenge:** action: name one enemy in sight; until your next turn it must target you or have disadvantage on its attacks.

### ϟ Wildcaller

- **Kit:** light armor, focus staff, flint, pouch of volatile reagents.
- **Archetype ability — Loose the Storm:** once per short rest, action: unleash a burst on a nearby zone; creatures there take `d6 + ϟ Zhark`, minus armor, and it leaves one fitting side effect on the zone.

<br>

- **Stormcaller**
  - **Chain Spark:** once per short rest, one attack also hits a second adjacent target for `d6`, minus armor.
- **Beastmaster**
  - **Leash:** once per short rest, a spell or creature you called obeys one simple command exactly as stated.
- **Whisperer**
  - **Talk Down:** when a creature you called turns on someone, a word from you stops it for a round.

### △ Delver

- **Kit:** light armor, pick or hammer (heavy), lantern, 50 feet of rope, chalk.
- **Archetype ability — Dig Through:** once per short rest, action: open or close a passage in earth, loose rock, or crude masonry up to about 5 feet thick, wide enough for one person, until your next rest. Solid bedrock and warded or metal barriers resist it.

<br>

- **Burrower**
  - **Tremorsense:** feel vibrations nearby; you sense what is moving in a nearby zone.
- **Brawler**
  - **Pit Fighter:** advantage on checks in cramped or underground spaces.
- **Sapper**
  - **Sound the Rock:** study a ceiling, wall, or floor closely; you know whether it will hold weight and for how long.

### △ Rootbound

- **Kit:** light armor, staff or club, seed pouch, water skin, herb bundle.
- **Archetype ability — Take Root:** once per short rest, action: through living plants, heal a nearby ally `d6` or give them 1 armor until your next turn.

<br>

  - **Slow Burn:** regain 1 extra HP whenever you finish a short rest.

  - **Boundary Seed:** once per short rest, action: plant a ring of seeds nearby; the first creature to cross it each turn must stop, then may continue next turn. The ring lasts one scene.

- **Archetype ability — Rally the Room:** once per short rest, reaction: give an ally advantage on their next check.

<br>

## Spells

Active alpha spells. Rules for casting, mana, and rests are in the guide.

- **Starting spells:** a new character knows 1 or 2 spells. More are learned through play.
- **Cost:** base mana cost in brackets. It drops by 1 if you cast with a Fount score of 4+ and by 2 at 6+, never below 1 (never below 2 for a base-3 spell).
- **Defaults:** range nearby (about 30 ft), duration 5 minutes. Larger scope, distance, duration, or unwilling targets are harder.
- **Stacking:** the same spell does not stack with itself; the newer replaces the older. Different spells stack, but spells never raise armor above 3.
- **Unwilling targets:** Charm, Babble, Boogie bomb, Improv sesh, and any other spell that controls, disables, or erases an unwilling creature follow the hard-cast rule in the guide.
- **Check:** see when to roll. A risky cast rolls `d20 + Fount` for the spell's Fount. A failed cast still spends its mana.

### ᛄ Seron

- **Force shield (2):** a bubble covers you and one nearby ally; damage to them is reduced by `d6` until your next turn.
- **Reveal path (1):** you know the direction and rough distance to anything or anyone you have seen in person or in a picture, even an unrecognizable one. It shows no obstacles or dangers, and wards can block it.
- **Seal (2):** name a door, container, or line; it cannot be opened or crossed until you leave or end it. Forcing it takes a hard check.
- **Ironbane (2):** for 5 minutes, the first iron weapon or tool to touch you disintegrates, then the spell ends. Iron bars, structures, and armor are not affected.
- **Cast fog (1):** fog in a color of your choice fills a nearby zone.
- **Telekinesis (2):** move unattended objects nearby, up to your **ᛄ** *Seron* score in number. Heavier or more numerous loads make it a risky check.

### ∵ Orvik

- **Simplify problem (1):** reveals a clue and makes one hard task risky instead.
- **Wizard mark (1):** touch to coat a target in paint only casters can see, even through walls.
- **Thaumaturgic anchor (2):** an enchanted item draws the next spell or magical effect aimed at something else nearby, then goes inert.
- **Illusion (2):** an illusion aimed at one of a target's five senses.
- **Mirror image (3):** physical clones of you appear, as many as your **∵** *Orvik* score, for 5 minutes. They have your mindset and act on their own, have your normal physical capabilities, cannot cast spells or use abilities that cost mana, and vanish if hit once. They carry no copy of your inventory, gear, or tools; clones cannot use consumables. They move and act independently, but only one clone can attack each round, dealing `d6 − 2` (minimum 1).
- **Question rock (1):** ask an object or non-verbal creature one question about what it has plausibly perceived. Only you hear the answer, and it is always true but may be partial.

### Cantrips

Cantrips are effortless expressions of a Fount. They cost no mana and need no roll, so they can be cast freely. Their effects are small, brief, and harmless: range self to 15 ft, duration 1 minute unless stated; effects that record or encode something last as stated. They cannot directly inflict or prevent damage, restore health, impose mechanical conditions, or compel another creature's actions. They cannot stack with themselves, bypass a significant obstacle, permanently change a creature, or do what a spell does. Names come from V1.

A cantrip can still have indirect results in the fiction: a small light may expose a hidden creature, and a noise may distract a guard. Cantrips manipulate details; spells change outcomes. A cantrip gives one useful way to approach a problem; it does not solve a whole encounter.
